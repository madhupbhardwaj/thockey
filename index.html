Set up in a minute

  1. Download and open Keytone

    Move it to Applications and open it. A keyboard icon appears in your menu bar.

  2. Allow keyboard access

    macOS asks before any app can hear keys pressed in other apps. Click Allow in the panel, then switch Keytone on in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility.

  3. Pick a sound

    Choose from the grid and type. Set the volume, and turn on Open at login if you want it every day.

Questions

What does Keytone do?

It plays a short sound every time you press a key, in any app. Pick the sound from the menu bar: mechanical keyboards, a typewriter, a quiet laptop tap, raindrops, bubbles or wood.

Does Keytone record what I type?

No. It uses only which key was pressed, to choose the sound and where it sits in stereo. It doesn’t save your typing and makes no network connections.

Why does it need Accessibility access?

macOS only lets an app notice key presses in other apps once you allow it under System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility. Without it, Keytone can only play sounds while its own panel is open.

Why is there no sound in password fields?

macOS hides key presses from every other app while you type a password. That’s a good thing, and it means Keytone stays quiet there.

Which sounds are based on real keyboards?

Thocky, Creamy, Poppy, Clacky and Clicky are fitted to recordings of real keyboards: the energy and decay of each key press were measured across 18 frequency bands and matched. The other sounds are modelled from how each one physically makes its sound.

Will it slow down my Mac?

Sounds are prepared once, in about a tenth of a second, when you choose a profile. After that each key press just plays a short clip that’s already in memory.

What do I need to run it?

A Mac running macOS 13 Ventura or later.

How do I uninstall it?

Turn off Open at login in the panel and quit Keytone. Remove it from System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility, then drag Keytone from Applications to the Bin.
